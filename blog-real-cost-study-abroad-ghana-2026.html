Planning and Costs

The real cost of studying abroad from Ghana in 2026.

By Alliance Educational Consult May 2026 10 min read

Families in Accra planning a study-abroad journey often work from incomplete numbers. They hear a tuition figure from a friend, add a rough estimate for living costs, and arrive at a total that bears little resemblance to what the actual expenditure will be. The shock of the real numbers — when they arrive — causes some to abandon the plan entirely and others to underprepare, which leads to financial stress once the student is already abroad.

This article gives actual 2026 numbers for the destinations Alliance supports most. These are not minimum figures or best-case scenarios. They are realistic estimates based on what Ghanaian students and their families are actually spending.

"The tuition is only the beginning. The families who plan well know the full number before they start."

Canada: first-year total cost estimate

🇨🇦

Canada — Diploma or PG Certificate (1 year)

Tuition (Georgian College / NIC Toronto)USD $14,000 – $18,000
Living costs (accommodation, food, transport)USD $12,000 – $15,000
Guaranteed Investment Certificate (GIC)CAD $20,635 (~USD $15,000)
Health insurance (UHIP or equivalent)USD $600 – $900
Study permit feeCAD $150 (~USD $110)
Biometrics feeCAD $85 (~USD $62)
Flight (Accra to Toronto return)USD $1,200 – $1,800
Realistic first-year totalUSD $43,000 – $51,000

The GIC is often the number that surprises families. It is not a cost in the traditional sense — it is a deposit that your student can access monthly once in Canada for living expenses. But it must be funded and held before the study permit is issued, which means you need access to approximately USD $15,000 in addition to the tuition and living costs evidence.

The UK is generally cheaper than Canada for postgraduate study when you account for program duration. A one-year UK Master's typically costs USD $10,000 to $20,000 less in total than the equivalent qualification taken over two years in Canada. If the student's goal is to return to Ghana or a third country after graduation, this difference is significant.

The costs that families frequently miss

Pre-departure setup costs

First and last month's rent deposit, bedding and household basics, a laptop if needed, winter clothing for Canada or northern UK. Budget USD $1,500 to $3,000 for setup costs not covered by regular living estimates.

Documentation and agent fees

Professional documentation preparation, SOP writing, application support, and agent fees. Quality varies widely. Alliance charges in milestones, not large upfront sums. Budget USD $800 to $2,500 depending on the complexity of the case.

Document authentication

Transcripts, certificates, and police clearance may need notarisation and authentication at the Ministry of Foreign Affairs. Allow GHS 300 to 800 per document and several weeks for processing.

Emergency buffer

Illness, travel disruption, delayed accommodation, a failed module requiring retaking — none of these are likely, but all of them happen. Families who send a student with no buffer create serious risk. Allow USD $2,000 to $3,000 held in reserve.

Scholarship options that genuinely reduce the cost

Several scholarship routes are realistic for Ghanaian students and worth pursuing seriously.

  • Institutional scholarships: Many Canadian colleges and UK universities offer merit-based awards of USD $1,000 to $5,000 that reduce tuition directly. Georgian College and NIC Toronto both have international student bursary programmes.
  • Commonwealth Scholarships: Available for postgraduate study in the UK. Competitive but open to Ghanaian nationals. Covers full tuition and living costs. Application is through the Ghana Scholarships Secretariat.
  • USA scholarship routes: For high-achieving students, US graduate assistantships and fellowships can effectively cover the full cost of a Master's or PhD program. This requires a very strong academic record and a well-targeted application strategy.
  • Government of Ghana scholarships: The Ghana Scholarship Secretariat manages several bilateral scholarship programmes. Check current offerings directly with them.

The planning timeline that prevents financial problems

The families who navigate study abroad financing most smoothly are those who start planning twelve to eighteen months before the intended intake. This gives time to build the required bank statement history organically, prepare financial documentation without rushing, explore scholarship options before deadlines pass, and apply in the early intake windows when institutional scholarship funds are still available.

Families who start three months before intake are already in a difficult position. They compress every part of the process and often face the choice between a rushed application or missing the intake entirely.
